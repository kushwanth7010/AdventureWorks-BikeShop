# Global Market Expansion & Profitability Strategy — AdventureWorks

## Executive Question
How should a global consumer-products business prioritize growth across markets, categories, pricing, and customer initiatives while protecting profitability?

## Evidence Base
The underlying fictional AdventureWorks analysis covers $24.9M revenue, $10.5M profit, 25,164 orders, 84,174 units, a 2.17% return rate, and 17,416 active customers across six countries.

## Structured Diagnosis
### 1. Market attractiveness
Compare countries using revenue scale, recent growth, customer depth, and concentration risk. Australia appears strategically important in the existing analysis because its revenue contribution is comparable with the combined United States market.

### 2. Portfolio economics
Bikes contribute the majority of revenue, while accessories contribute higher unit volume at lower price points. This creates both concentration risk and a cross-sell opportunity.

### 3. Customer growth
Assess whether growth comes from new customers, repeat customers, higher order value, or pricing. A growth plan should specify which mechanism is expected to create value.

### 4. Pricing and mix
Use controlled what-if analysis to test price changes by category rather than applying uniform increases. Track both revenue and margin because volume response can offset price gains.

### 5. Execution
Prioritize a small number of commercial experiments:
- cross-sell accessories to bike purchasers,
- market-specific pricing tests,
- retention campaigns for high-value customer segments,
- product-level return reduction actions.

## Recommendations
1. Defend the strongest markets before entering additional geographies.
2. Reduce revenue concentration by increasing attachment of accessories and complementary products to bike orders.
3. Use market- and category-specific price tests rather than a global price increase.
4. Track incremental profit, not only incremental revenue.
5. Review results monthly using revenue growth, contribution margin, average order value, repeat purchase, attachment rate, and return rate.

## Risks and Caveats
- AdventureWorks is fictional sample data.
- Market-entry decisions normally require external market size, competitor, regulatory, distribution, and investment data that are not present here.
- Historic sales correlations do not prove that a proposed commercial action will cause growth.

## Why This Is a Consulting Project
The work reframes BI output into a strategy question, identifies growth levers, separates revenue from profit, proposes experiments, and defines KPIs and risks.
